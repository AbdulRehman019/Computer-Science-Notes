✅ 1. Flashcards (Quick Recall Style)
	Use these to quiz yourself fast.

	Q: When should you think of binary search or priority queue?
	A: When your brute force gives linear time (O(n)) but needs to be optimized to log(n) or constant.

	Q: When do you use Opposite End Pointers?
	A: When comparing/extremes, sorted input, or when squeezing the array from both ends.

	Q: What’s a sliding window used for?
	A: When expanding and shrinking a window (subarray/substring) to meet a constraint.

	Q: When do you use prefix sum + map?
	A: When you're counting subarrays with specific sums or need constant-time subarray sum checks.

	Q: What does Binary Search on Answer mean?
	A: Search over a range of possible numeric answers (e.g., min-max values), using binary search logic.

	Q: When do you use merge sort in place of sorting?
	A: When you can't sort due to index dependency but still need sorted-like behavior (e.g., count inversions).

	Q: When should map or set pop into your mind?
	A: When you wish to check for past presence or associations of elements efficiently.

	Q: What trick helps in rotating or shifting arrays without extra space?
	A: The reverse technique (reverse parts, then whole array).
	
	Q: What if you wish an array was sorted but can’t sort it?
	A: Use merge sort-based strategy during traversal (e.g., count inversions, reverse pairs).

🧩 3. Quick 5-Line Intuition Summaries
	🔁 Sliding Window
		Use two pointers to form a window. Expand right to add elements. Shrink left when constraints are broken.
		Useful for substring or subarray conditions. Keeps window valid at all times.

	↔ Opposite Pointers
		When comparing outer elements — typically sorted arrays. Move inward based on a condition.
		Often used for max/min pairing, palindrome, two-sum.

	📊 Prefix Sum + Map
		Track running totals and their occurrences. Helps to count subarrays that match sum constraints.
		Think “how much excess do I have now?” → check map for past prefix.

	🔍 Binary Search on Answer
		Your answer is a number within a range. Binary search this number range.
		Check feasibility for mid and decide direction.

	🧠 Merge Sort Trick
		When index order matters but you need sorted behavior — use merge sort.
		Track and count while merging (e.g., inversion count, reverse pair count).
