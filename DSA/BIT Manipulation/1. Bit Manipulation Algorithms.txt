1. Convert Decimal to Binary
	Divide the decimal number by 2 until it becomes 1 and add remainder in your answer everytime. 
	Once, your decimal number reaches 1. Come out of the loop and reverse the answer. 

	Time Complexity: O(log(n))
	Space Complexity: O(log(n))

2. Convert Binary to Decimal
	Take power = 1. Start from right and move to left. Multiply power by 2 everytime. If character at that position is 1, add power. 

	Time Complexity: O(length of string)
	Space Complexity: O(1) 

3. 1's compliment
	Reverse all the flips of binary representation of decimal numbers. 

4. 2's compliment 
	Reverse all the flips of binary representation of decimal numbers and add 1. 

5. Operators 
	AND: (13 & 7) = (1101 & 0111) = (0101) = 5
	OR: (13 | 7) = (1101 & 0111) = (1111) = 15
	XOR: (13 ^ 7) = (1101 ^ 0111) = (1010) = 10
	SHIFT: Right Shift: (a >> b) = (a / (2^b))
	       Left Shift: (a << b) = (a * (2^b)) 
	NOT: First flip all the bits 
	     If it is negative, it stores 2's compliment. 
	     If it is positive, it stops. 

	     Hence, if the number is positive it stores 2's compliment of the flipped bits. 
	     If the number is negative it stores the flipped bits i.e. 1's compliment of the number. 

6. How computer stores positive and negative integers? 
	It use MSB to decide positive and negative numbers. 
	Positive: It stores positive number by making MSB as 0. 
	Negative: It stores negative number by making MSB as 1. It stores the 2's compliment of positive number. 

7. Largest and Smallest Number an integer can hold?
	Largest: Since, we need to exclude MSB. Hence, largest number it can hold is (2^31 - 1). 
	Smallest: Here, we can use MSB to store negative value. Hence, it can hold (2^31).
